# Brand strategy

A brand strategy is a set of choices that make every later piece of work
easier: who we serve, what we are instead of, why we win, what proves it,
and how we sound. Build it from the brief and the research, never from three
adjectives picked out of the air.

## 1. Positioning

Answer each with evidence from the brief or the research (cite sources):

| Question | What a good answer looks like |
|---|---|
| **Best customers** | The people who already love it and why (from reviews, repeat buyers, the owner's knowledge) |
| **Real alternatives** | What they would do without the user: a named competitor, doing it themselves, a different kind of product, doing nothing |
| **What is different** | Concrete things the alternatives do not do or do worse, observed, not claimed |
| **Value** | What the difference gets the customer, in their words |
| **Category** | The frame that makes the value obvious ("neighbourhood chai stop", not "beverage solutions") |

Write the positioning in two sentences: for <who>, <brand> is the
<category> that <value>, unlike <alternative> which <limit>. Then a
plain-language version a customer would say to a friend.

## 2. Message

- **One promise** the brand keeps everywhere.
- **Three supporting messages**, each with its proof: a fact from the brief
  (price, result, rating, years, numbers of customers), a review quote, or a
  demonstration. No proof, no message.
- **Objections and answers:** the 3 to 5 reasons people hesitate (from the
  research) and how the brand answers each honestly.
- **Customer words:** phrases buyers use for the problem and for the brand,
  copied from reviews and forums. Use them in copy instead of company words.

## 3. Voice and tone

Voice is how the brand always sounds; tone is how much of it to turn up in
a given moment.

1. Collect samples: the brand's best-performing posts, the owner's own
   writing, how staff explain the product, review language, and one
   competitor's copy that feels wrong (it shows the edges).
2. Write 3 to 5 voice traits. Each needs **means**, **do**, **avoid**,
   and a real example line. "Avoid" does the work: "friendly" that does not
   rule out "over-familiar" changes nothing.
3. Tone by moment: social posts (most personality), offers and launches
   (energy from specifics, not adjectives), replies to complaints (calm,
   own the problem in the first line), prices and policies (exact, plain).
4. Word lists: terms the brand owns (always the same name for the same
   thing), customer words, words never to use with the reason.
5. Test it: if a line would read the same with a competitor's name in it,
   it is not the brand's voice yet.

Match the language(s) the brand's customers use, as given in the brief:
write natively in that language rather than translating English idioms.

## 4. Look (when the brief has no brand kit)

Suggest colours and type from the brand's mood and its competitors (stand
apart from the main competitor's colours), and keep it simple: one main
colour, one accent, one typeface family. If the user has a logo, take the
colours from it.

## Output

For the strategy report: a `strategy` or `positioning` section (text and
cards), a `voice` section (a `voice` block with traits and 2 to 4 sample
lines), and the proof points as facts. Every claim of difference is
something observed or given by the user, never invented.
