# Copywriting

## Before writing

Know four things (from the brief and the brand strategy): what this piece is
for and the one action it asks for; who reads it and what they already
know; the offer, with its real facts; where it appears (a feed, an inbox, a
search result, a shelf).

## Principles

- **Clear beats clever.** If a reader has to work out what you mean, rewrite.
- **Benefits through specifics.** "Fresh chai made in front of you in clay
  cups" beats "an authentic tea experience".
- **The customer's words.** Use the phrases buyers use (from reviews and
  forums), not company words.
- **One idea per piece or section.**
- **Every claim supportable.** Prices, numbers, results, ratings and quotes
  come from the user's facts or a source. If a claim cannot be backed, cut
  it or say what would back it. No "guaranteed", "best in the world",
  "number one", health or money promises the facts cannot prove.
- **Native language.** Write directly in the language and register of the
  market in the brief; do not translate English idioms word for word. Use
  the local currency, date style and units.

## Structure

Headline that says the value; a line that makes it concrete; proof (fact,
review, demonstration); the answer to the main objection; one clear call to
action that says what happens next.

## Sounds human, not generated

Never write:
- contrast reveals ("It's not X, it's Y", "Not because X. Because Y.")
- negation lists ("No X, no Y, no Z.")
- self-answered questions and colon reveals ("The result? 3x faster.")
- stock phrases: unlock, elevate, seamless, game-changer, revolutionize,
  take it to the next level, in today's fast-paced world, say goodbye to,
  look no further, we've got you covered
- engagement bait ("Agree?", "Thoughts?", "Read that again")
- a sentence per line for a whole post; emoji as bullets; em dashes

Keep rare: lists of three, fragments, rhetorical questions, exclamation marks.

Check before delivering: read it aloud as the brand's best salesperson
would say it to one customer. Anything they would not say, cut.
